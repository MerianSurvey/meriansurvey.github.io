Survey

Medium bands over a deep broad-band sky

Merian adds two medium-band filters to the five broad bands of the Hyper Suprime-Cam Subaru Strategic Program. The combination gives seven-band photometry and photometric redshifts for dwarf galaxies across well-studied equatorial fields.

Filters

N540 and N708

The central wavelengths and widths were chosen to catch [O III] and Hα from galaxies with stellar masses of 10⁸ to 10⁹ solar masses, by comparing the medium-band fluxes with the HSC broad-band fluxes.

The design balanced two requirements, the signal-to-noise of the weak lensing measurement and the performance of the photometric redshifts. Simulations made for the design predicted a photometric redshift accuracy of about 0.01 in Δz/(1+z), with an outlier fraction of 2.8%, over 0.058 < z < 0.10.

Fields

Wide and deep

Merian-Wide

About 750 deg² on the equator

Two large contiguous regions inside the HSC-SSP Wide layer, overlapping the GAMA, COSMOS, VVDS, XMM-LSS and SXDS fields. Both filters cover about 733 deg², of which about 584 deg² reaches full depth in both.

Merian-Deep

One pointing in COSMOS

A single DECam pointing of about 2 deg² with roughly ten times the observing time of Merian-Wide. It is used to characterize the selection function and to test photometry and galaxy shape measurements.

Data processing

Seven bands on one pixel grid

Merian images are reduced with the LSST Science Pipelines. The coadded DECam images are resampled onto the same pixel grid as the HSC-SSP images, so that all seven bands can be measured together.

Galaxy colors come from Gaussian Aperture and PSF (GAaP) photometry, which matches apertures and point spread functions across images taken with two different telescopes. Accurate colors are what make accurate photometric redshifts possible.

The photometric redshifts are calibrated and validated with spectra from SDSS and GAMA and with new spectroscopy of candidate dwarf galaxies in the COSMOS field.
